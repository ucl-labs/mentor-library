# The Six-Pillar Reputation System

> **North star for off-site authority.** This is the canonical model UpClickLabs and its clients
> build toward. Every off-site authority engagement should be scorable, pillar by pillar, against
> this list. Source: Neil Patel (NP Digital), "AI isn't ranking pages anymore" talk.

## The core claim

Google gave you a **list**. AI gives you a **verdict**.

For 20 years the job was to win one of ten blue links and let the buyer compare. AI search reads
the options for the buyer, weighs the sources against each other, and returns a conclusion naming
two or three businesses. Everyone else is left out of the conversation, and the buyer never learns
you were an option.

So the unit of optimisation is no longer the page. It is the **verdict** the machine writes about
your business after compressing everything ever published about you into four sentences.

## The six pillars

| # | Pillar | What it is | What proves it exists |
|---|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Content that establishes what we believe** | A stated point of view, not topic coverage | Published opinion pieces, a documented methodology, a named position |
| 2 | **A website that says exactly who we are** | Unambiguous, consistent self-description | Homepage / about / services copy that matches every off-site surface |
| 3 | **Real names attached to real expertise** | Named humans, publicly evidenced | Bylines, author bios, speaker pages, LinkedIn profiles tied to the work |
| 4 | **Case studies that document what we've done** | Outcome evidence, specific and dated | Published case studies with named clients, numbers, timeframes |
| 5 | **Reviews that prove the experience** | Third-party verified customer voice | G2, Trustpilot, Google Business Profile, industry directories |
| 6 | **Original research that gives other people a reason to cite us** | Citable primary data | Surveys, benchmark reports, proprietary datasets others quote |

**The unifying rule:** not nine channels with nine goals. One story, told everywhere, that a machine
can verify from any angle.

## Why these six (the evidence)

NP Digital analysed **82 factors** to determine what gets a brand recommended by ChatGPT:

| Rank | Factor | Note |
|------|--------|------|
| 1 | Relevancy | Expected |
| 2 | **Brand mentions** | Nearly tied with relevancy. How often you are talked about across sites you do not own |
| 3 | Reviews | Maps to pillar 5 |
| 4 | Authority | Maps to pillars 3, 4, 6 |

Scored across **six different AI platforms**:

- **Third-party citations** scored high on *every single platform tested*. This is the one universal signal.
- **Expert authorship** scored **4+ out of 5 across every platform**. This is pillar 3 and it is not optional.
- **Backlinks scored 1.9 out of 5 on ChatGPT**, near the bottom of the list.

> The most valuable currency of the old internet barely registers on the new one. What replaces it is
> *evidence about you*: real names on content, independent press, reviews, other trusted sources
> vouching for you.

Corroboration already in this KB: `blog/150-plus-ai-seo-statistics-for-2026.md` records that brands
are **6.5x more likely to be cited through third-party sources than their own domains**. Two
independent sources, same conclusion.

## Perplexity's trust labels (the tiers made visible)

Perplexity now labels sources inside its answers, and states *why* each label was earned. Four levels:

| Level | Label | Typically earned by |
|-------|-------|--------------------|
| 1 | (no label) | Most sources |
| 2 | Credible | Reasonable track record |
| 3 | Trusted | Specialised industry publication with a strong track record in that field |
| 4 | Authoritative | Primary source: government sites, a company's own technical documentation. The origin of the information, not a copy of it |

It also flags political lean of a source.

**Why this matters:** for 20 years the ranking algorithm was a black box you reverse-engineered from
outside. Perplexity is now showing its trust map on screen. ChatGPT and Gemini are making the same
judgment about your business, they just do not print the badge. The lesson is that these systems run
a background check on *who said it*, not only on what the page says.

**Actionable read:** pillar 6 (original research) is the fastest route to the "authoritative" tier,
because it makes you a primary source rather than a copy.

## Compression is brutal

AI pulls your website, founder LinkedIn, old interviews, podcasts, reviews, five-year-old press, and
Reddit threads you have never read, then compresses it into one paragraph. That paragraph becomes the
first thing a prospect learns about you, before the site, before sales.

Whatever survives compression becomes the story. For most businesses what survives is a mess: the
site says one thing, the founder profile says another, the press covers a product pivoted away from
in 2021, and the team's real expertise has zero public evidence attached to any name.

A human untangles that with judgment. The machine does not fill gaps. **Muddy signals in, muddy
paragraph out**, or worse, a confident paragraph that is flat out wrong about what you do.

## The consistency trap (why org charts break this)

Most businesses run website, ads, social, PR, and reviews as separate functions with different teams
and different goals, each presenting a slightly different version of the company. That was survivable
when humans evaluated each channel on its own merits.

It is dangerous now because **the machine reads all of it as one document**. Every inconsistency
between channels is noise in the machine's understanding of what business you are in. A confused
machine matches you with the wrong audience, generates the wrong message, and recommends your
competitor to your customer.

This extends into paid: Google's AI-powered ad products read your site, landing page, product info,
and creative to decide what you offer, who to match it with, and in some cases what your ad should
say. Your website stopped being the destination an ad points at. It is now source material the
machine studies before it builds the campaign.

## The failure mode to avoid

The instinct on hearing all this is to **produce more**: more blog posts, more AI-generated pages,
more channels. That is the wrong lesson.

> You cannot out-publish this. Publishing more of a contradictory story just makes the contradiction
> louder. The play is **fewer, stronger signals**, every piece of proof making the same story clearer
> and easier to verify.

## The commercial case

Visitors arriving from AI recommendations convert about **8x more than traditional traffic**, and
they convert faster. By the time they land, the AI has already vetted, compared, and recommended you.
They are not browsing, they arrive pre-sold. This is the highest-intent traffic that exists, and a
rankings dashboard cannot see any of it.

## The homework (client-facing opener)

**Ask an AI to describe the business today. Read the four sentences carefully.**

This is the single best discovery exercise for an off-site authority pitch. It converts an abstract
problem into a document the prospect can read, dislike, and want fixed. Most competitors have never
read theirs.

## How to use this in delivery

1. **Audit:** score the client on all six pillars, marking each present / partial / absent with the URL evidence.
2. **Diagnose:** the lowest-scoring pillar is the first work order, not the easiest one.
3. **Sequence:** pillar 3 (real names on real expertise) and pillar 6 (original research) carry the
   heaviest weight in the platform data, so they outrank generic content volume.
4. **Never** argue for backlinks as the primary AI-visibility lever. The data puts them at 1.9/5 on ChatGPT.
5. **Refresh:** see `youtube/zero-click-marketing-microconf-amanda-natividad.md` (Seer Interactive case).
   Corrections to the public record decay. One negative review theme resurfaced 67 times in branded
   AI outputs, and publishing counter-evidence only suppressed it while the data was kept fresh.
