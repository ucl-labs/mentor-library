# The AI Discovery Playbook (Webflow x HubSpot)

**One line:** AEO splits into four pillars - content, technical, authority, measurement - and the
median org scores 2/5 on maturity, held back by fundamentals, not by advanced AI tactics.

Contributors: Guy Yalif (Chief Evangelist, Webflow), Aja Frost (Sr. Director Global Growth & Paid,
HubSpot), Dave Steer (CMO, Webflow), Vivian Hoang (AEO/SEO Lead, Webflow), Alexander Diner (Head of
Brand Studio, Webflow), plus customer voices from AstroPay, IMB Bank, OysterHR, Net-Inspect.

---

## 1. The core reframe

Traditional demand gen: **traffic -> leads -> pipeline.**
AI-era demand gen: **AI visibility -> informed buyers -> leads -> pipeline.**

Buyers now arrive further down the funnel. Webflow reports **6x higher conversion** from AI-referred
visitors vs historical non-branded organic. HubSpot paused normal work, ran experiments across every
lever, scaled what worked, and grew **qualified leads from AI search 1,850%**, becoming the most
visible CRM in AI engines.

Three priorities for marketing leaders:
1. **Narrative accuracy** - your positioning must appear consistently across the sources AI uses.
2. **Measurement** - buyers form preferences before they reach the site; you need to see that.
3. **Differentiation** - AI flattens category sameness; specific positioning gives it something to grab.

> "Someone found us on ChatGPT and said our price was half of what it actually is (it was a page from
> 2021)." - Josephine Cahill, Web Lead, OysterHR

## 2. The AEO Maturity Model (Webflow) - the headline framework

Five levels x four pillars. Use this to score a client and pick the next move.

| | **L1 Keywords** | **L2 Answers** | **L3 Structure** | **L4 Pillar** | **L5 Authority** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | Keyword-focused content | Some answer-oriented content | Answering question clusters | Answer-hierarchy-driven content | Programmatic AEO with personalized content |
| **Technical** | On-page keyword SEO | Some basic page structure | Consistent structure on a fast site | Automated site structure | MCP and early standards adoption |
| **Authority** | Backlinks | Active Google E-E-A-T | Proactive digital PR, Reddit, thought leadership | Recognized pillar of thought leadership with engaging visual storytelling | Widespread, positive citations |
| **Measurement** | Ranking for target keywords | Ad hoc mention checking | Systematically tracking LLM traffic, mentions, sentiment | Share of voice and accuracy tracking | Real-time analytics |

### Benchmark: where everyone actually sits

Webflow analysed **2,000+ websites (Jul '26)**. Median overall AEO maturity = **2 out of 5**.

| Pillar | Median score |
|---|---|
| Overall | 2 |
| Content | 1 |
| Technical | 1 |
| Authority | 2 |
| Measurement | 3 |

**Read:** the gap is in content and technical fundamentals, not exotic AI tactics.

### What's holding brands back (% of analysed sites with the gap)

*New AEO fundamentals*
- **73%** - lack of widespread mentions (mentioned on under 25% of the sites AI cites for their category)
- **57%** - not answering prospects' questions (content not deliberately built around buyer questions)
- **55%** - hiding topic authority (no author byline on 85%+ of pages that need one)

*Historical fundamentals*
- **62%** - broken internal links (10%+ of internal links lead nowhere)
- **61%** - illogical heading structure (broken H1 -> H2 -> H3 on 40%+ of pages)
- **60%** - missing SEO metadata (missing titles or descriptions on 20%+ of pages)
- **54%** - stale content (under 10% of content updated in the past six months)

## 3. Pillar 1 - Content: answer questions, not keywords

SEO queries were often 4 words. AEO questions run 20+ words and come in connected chains. Example for
"headless CMS": what is it -> when should a regulated company use one -> what are the tradeoffs -> how
does it compare to traditional CMS -> what implementation challenges should a subsidiary expect.

**Mine questions from the business, not a keyword tool.** Sales objections, customer success
workflows, support tickets. IMB Bank builds content straight off retail network and contact centre
question logs.

> "We don't create content to rank. We create content to solve." - Juan Felipe Valderrama Vargas,
> Head of Growth and User Acquisition, Astropay

**Refresh beats net-new.** 95% of ChatGPT citations reference pages updated within the previous 10
months (Novastacks AI). Patterns that consistently help:
- Lead with a direct answer
- Question-based headings
- Comparison tables where buyers evaluate options
- FAQs where they fit naturally
- Original examples, data, and customer stories with citations

**Webflow test - table of contents:** Vivian Hoang added TOCs to blog posts. Within four weeks those
posts saw **+59% AI-sourced traffic** and **+23% SEO traffic**.

**Case - AstroPay:** after SEO fundamentals + AEO, went from near zero to **2.5M unique monthly
visitors**, appearing in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and AI Overviews alongside Wise and Western Union.

### Diagnostic: which content problem is yours?

| **Accelerate the machine** | **Earn the mention** |
|---|---|
| You have content, it just hasn't been refreshed. Hundreds of pages untouched in a year; fragmented ownership; publishing needs many handoffs. | You have expertise, it just isn't packaged so AI can reference it. Articles target keywords not questions; product pages lack comparisons or FAQs. |
| **Focus first:** refresh workflows before net-new production. | **Focus first:** answer-first structures that are easy to compare and cite. |

## 4. Pillar 2 - Technical: make expertise findable

Four components: **schema, site architecture, crawlability, performance.**

- **73% of page-one results use schema markup, but 88% of sites don't use schema at all.**
- Rotten Tomatoes reported a **25% CTR increase** after implementing structured data.
- Technical improvements at Webflow contributed to **485% QoQ growth in LLM-driven signups.**

> "If your most important content only appears after an interaction fires, as far as a machine's
> concerned, it essentially does not exist." - Alexander Diner, Head of Brand Studio, Webflow

Content present on the page from the start. Fewer reveals, fewer gates. It made the page more direct
for humans too.

**Five foundational questions:**
1. Can AI access your most important content without human interaction?
2. Is your expertise clearly organized across topics?
3. Do pages explain relationships between ideas?
4. Are important pages easy to find?
5. Can marketing ship improvements quickly?

### Diagnostic: which technical problem is yours?

| **Unblock the queue** | **Automate the foundation** |
|---|---|
| You know what to fix but can't ship. Marketing depends on engineering for basic updates; fixes take weeks; publishing velocity has slowed. | Your technical work is too manual to scale. Schema inconsistent across pages; metadata hand-managed; technical debt grows quarterly. |
| **Focus first:** give marketers autonomy to update the site. | **Focus first:** build systems that make technical best practice repeatable. |

## 5. Pillar 3 - Authority beyond your website

> "For high-intent queries, more than half of AI citations come from Reddit, LinkedIn, and YouTube. AI
> is using these sources to both form and validate opinions. We stopped thinking of LinkedIn as a pure
> brand awareness play and started treating it as a citation lever." - Aja Frost, HubSpot

**Webflow FAQ + schema test:** added FAQ sections (reformatting info already on the page) plus schema
to **six core product pages**. Within two weeks: **330+ new citations**; across a ~250,000-page site
those **six pages generated 57% of all new citations**; **+24% organic impressions.**

### Channel playbook

| Channel | Why it matters | One move to make |
|---|---|---|
| **Reddit / Q&A forums** | Buyers ask brutally specific "what's the catch" questions; threads combine real-world language, objections, and consensus | Identify the top 5-10 evergreen threads in your category ("best [category] tools", "[Tool A] vs [Tool B]"). Have community/support/advocacy monitor and participate where your brand isn't accurately represented |
| **LinkedIn** | Posts and articles from credible experts are scraped and treated as high-authority inputs, especially with engagement | Publish 2-3 flagship posts from a named expert explaining your category, what you're best for, and how you differ. Make company tagline + About section use the same language so AI can triangulate |
| **YouTube** | Buyers use it to see products in practice; **cited in over 15% of LLM answers** | Create 2-3 evergreen videos answering common buyer questions: comparisons, implementation guides, use-case walkthroughs. Optimize titles/descriptions in buyer language |
| **Industry pubs, review sites, podcasts, customer stories** | AI leans on independent validation - G2, trusted publications, podcast appearances | Audit where your category is already discussed, build a consistent presence, encourage reviews, turn customer outcomes into referenceable stories |

**E-E-A-T still the foundation:** Experience (customer stories, practitioner insight), Expertise
(original perspectives), Authoritativeness (ideas others reference), Trustworthiness (transparent
sources, authorship, claims). Design counts too - **94% of first impressions are design related**, and
both humans and LLMs take cues from usability and presentation.

### Diagnostic: which authority problem is yours?

| **Own the answer** | **Control the narrative** |
|---|---|
| Expertise exists but stays on owned channels. SMEs rarely publish externally; customer stories live only on your site; competitors appear in AI answers more than expected. | People talk about your brand but the story is inconsistent. Third-party sites describe you differently; outdated info still surfaces; competitors are defining your category. |
| **Focus first:** expand presence in the channels buyers use to validate. | **Focus first:** audit the information ecosystem and align the story everywhere. |

## 6. Pillar 4 - Measurement: visibility to revenue

At Webflow: **8% of total new self-serve signups originate directly from LLMs**; **42% of non-brand
organic signups are attributed to AI**.

Buyer-side data: G2 found **51% of B2B buyers start research with an AI chatbot** (up from 29% a year
earlier) and **69% chose a different vendor** than planned based on chatbot guidance. HubSpot research
found AI search was the **single strongest predictor of purchase intent**, ahead of demos, review
sites, and sales calls - **42% of CRM buyers used AI search during evaluation and were 36% more likely
to purchase**. Webflow's own survey: **93% of marketing leaders say AEO is critical, only 25%
understand it.**

### The five metrics

| Metric | What it tracks | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Brand visibility | Whether your brand appears in AI answers | Baseline detectability |
| Citations | How often your site is cited in responses | Your authority in AI |
| Prominence of mentions | How prominently your brand appears | Depth of the mention |
| Sentiment | Positive / negative / neutral tone | Accuracy of your brand story |
| Share of voice | How often you're mentioned vs competitors | Competitive positioning |

**Why you must track competitors alongside yourself:** AI answers are non-deterministic - odds of
getting the same recommendation list twice are under **1 in 100**; the same list in the same order,
roughly **1 in 1,000**. Without competitor context you can't tell a drop you caused from a drop the
platform caused.

> "Now we track 40 buyer-intent prompts monthly, and that data shapes our content strategy the same way
> Google rankings do." - Kody VanSistine, Senior Product Manager, Net-Inspect

### Diagnostic: which measurement problem is yours?

| **Build the baseline** | **Compound the advantage** |
|---|---|
| You don't know how visible you are. No idea which platforms mention you; competitor visibility unclear; reporting is still traffic and rankings. | You have traction and need to tie it to outcomes. AI-referred traffic rising; buyers mention AI on sales calls; leadership wants proof of business impact. |
| **Focus first:** benchmark inclusion, citations, sentiment, prominence, share of voice. | **Focus first:** connect visibility metrics to contacts, opportunities, and revenue. |

## 7. Start small - the operating rhythm

> "Your AEO team is your SEO team. Your AEO agency is your SEO agency."

**3 things anyone can do this week**
1. Add FAQs to high-value pages.
2. Refresh five existing pages.
3. Audit your Google Knowledge Panel and AI descriptions for outdated or inconsistent info.

**The ramp**

| Horizon | Move | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Tonight** | Audit your AI presence | Query ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity manually; document where you're missing or misrepresented. Tools: Webflow/HubSpot AEO, Profound |
| **This week** | Strengthen the AI foundation | Schema, structured Q&A, clear factual claims. Publish answers to highest-value buyer questions. Tools: Search Console, Schema Markup Validator |
| **This month** | Measure what drives revenue | Add citation tracking, share of voice, AI-referred sessions to reporting; connect into CRM. Tools: GA4, Profound, Scrunch AI |
| **This quarter** | Turn AEO into an operating rhythm | Repeatable workflows for prompt monitoring, content refresh, citation tracking, cross-team coordination. Treat AI visibility as an ongoing growth program, not a one-time SEO project |

**Find your starting point by role**

| Role | Focus on | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing leader | Measurement and alignment | How does AI describe us? Where do competitors appear? Do we have shared success metrics? |
| Content / growth marketer | Existing content | Which pages haven't been updated? Which buyer questions are unanswered? Which assets already drive meaningful traffic? |
| Website experience | Execution speed | Which improvements are waiting on engineering? What technical work is repetitive? Where do small changes get stuck? |
| Brand / comms | Consistency | How is our company described externally? Are our differentiators clear? Where are buyers validating decisions? |
