# Flexible Sampling Guidelines

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/flexible-sampling

Google has developed experimental guidance for publishers implementing flexible sampling strategies. The organization conducted experiments with publishing partners to understand how sampling changes affect user experience and article ranking in search results.

## Two Sampling Approaches

1. **Metering**: Provides users with a quota of articles before requiring subscription or login, after which paywalls appear.

2. **Lead-in**: Offers the opening portion of an article without showing full content, allowing users to experience value before hitting a paywall.

## Metering Recommendations

### Timeframe
"Monthly, rather than daily metering provides more flexibility and a safer environment." Monthly approaches distribute paywall views among most-engaged users while allowing newer users to experience content value.

### Content Volume
For daily news publishers, Google expects optimal sampling "to fall between 6 and 10 articles per user per month." The guidance recommends starting at 10 articles monthly and adjusting based on analysis of current search-user paywall encounter rates.

## Lead-in Benefits

Showing initial article sentences above the paywall "let users experience the value of the content" while generating curiosity about the complete story, potentially supporting conversion efforts.

## Important Considerations

User studies indicate that "general user satisfaction starts to degrade significantly when paywalls are shown more than 10% of the time." Publishers should approach this threshold cautiously to avoid alienating users unconvinced of content value.

Advanced publishers may target sampling restrictions specifically toward highly-engaged users while allowing more liberal access for others.

## Implementation

Publishers should use structured data to indicate paywalled content, distinguishing it from cloaking practices where Googlebot receives different content than users.
