# Discover and your website

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover

Google's Discover feature displays content based on user interests and Web/App Activity.

## How Content Appears in Discover

Content automatically qualifies for Discover if it's indexed by Google and meets content policies - no special markup required. The feature shows diverse topics matching individual interests, including older content if relevant.

Violating Discover policies triggers manual actions visible in Search Console. Discover uses similar ranking signals as Google Search, so the guidance on "creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" applies here.

## Recommendations for Discover Success

- **Avoid clickbait**: Don't use misleading titles, exaggerated details, or sensationalism
- **Quality titles**: Use headlines capturing content essence
- **Timely content**: Provide current, well-told, or uniquely insightful material
- **High-quality images**: Include compelling visuals meeting these specs:
  - Minimum 1200 px wide
  - 300,000+ total pixels
  - 16:9 aspect ratio
  - Enabled via `max-image-preview:large` or AMP
- **Image specification**: Use schema.org markup or `og:image` meta tags; avoid generic logos or text-heavy images
- **Page experience**: Ensure overall site quality per Google's page experience guidelines

Discover avoids job applications, petitions, forms, code repositories, and unsourced satirical content.

## Traffic Variability

Discover traffic is less predictable than keyword-driven search because:
- User interests shift over time
- Discover adjusts content types continuously
- Google Search updates affect Discover as an extension of Search

## Monitoring Performance

The Search Console Performance report for Discover tracks impressions, clicks, and click-through rates over the last 16 months.
