# Why You Shouldn't Wait for Perfect AI Search Measurement (with Bain & Company)

> **RELIABILITY (2026-10-16).** A vendor webinar with a Bain partner on the panel. The Bain survey stats are
> credible but quoted second-hand from slides we have not seen. The conversion and undercount numbers are
> the vendor's summary of other people's studies. Use for **how to pitch and structure AEO measurement**,
> not as hard benchmarks. Re-verify any number before it goes in a client report.

**One line:** Nobody has dollar-level ROI for AI search yet, and CFOs don't expect it. Leaders invest
anyway, on strategic priority plus leading indicators. Set up measurement properly now so you can prove
ROI later.

---

## 1. The measurement gap is normal for a new channel

- Every new channel follows the same curve: **data arrives, then a method forms around it, then investment
  follows.** Google Search did this through Search Console and Analytics. Social did it in the 2010s.
- AI search is stuck at step one. The platforms have not released much data yet. That is a gap the market
  has to close itself, and it is very early in the wave.
- Bain's long-running finding: what separates leaders from laggards is **the speed of experiments and
  decisions**, not having clean data. Leaders decide quickly with whatever data they have.

## 2. What CFOs actually want

- In Bain's survey, CFOs only fully trust one function: finance. Marketing is not at the bottom. HR is.
- **CFOs don't expect perfection.** They expect thoughtfulness, clear baselines, a plan of what you'll do
  and what you expect from it, and improvement in the right direction.
- **Most CFOs want growth, not ROI.** ROI is an efficiency metric. CFOs will manage costs themselves. If
  AI search is a growth lever, they will fund it.
- Closing line from Bain: don't be afraid of your CFO. Partner with finance to build the method together.

## 3. Why leaders invest before the ROI is clear

- Leaders invest because AI has been named a **top-down strategic priority**, with a central roadmap,
  dedicated resources and freedom to move budget. It isn't because they've proven the dollar value.
- Only about **30%** measure AI impact with both marketing metrics and business metrics. The rest use
  one kind, or nothing.
- Laggards are about **2x more likely** to say unclear ROI and budget limits are holding them back.
- Leaders are about **2x more likely** than laggards to move spend toward AI search. Their first move is
  to max out the channels they own, because owned channels are the fastest thing they control.
- Money usually comes out of flexible demand gen or working media budgets.

## 4. How to fund AI search without breaking demand gen

- Bain has changed its advice. It used to say cast a wide net and run lots of pilots. Now it says
  **commit to a few specific use cases.** The companies doing that get more value.
- **Don't switch off the demand gen engine** to bet blind on AI search. There is only so much money you
  can spend there productively today.
- Hold a high bar on where the money comes from. Mature teams use **marginal ROI curves** on existing
  channels to see what they lose by taking money out.
- Risk depends on margins. A low-margin DTC brand can't easily switch off Meta to fund organic.

## 5. Two camps: win first vs prove first

| | Win first | Prove first |
|---|---|---|
| Belief | Being the AI's first choice is a company-level priority. Early investment may get baked into model memory. | We'll experiment, but we can't scale spend until we see dollars flow through our existing attribution. |
| Who | CEO-led, competitive, focused on the catalog | Equally sophisticated teams, often enterprise |
| Risk | Spreading activity thinly across every AEO lever and never being able to measure any of it | Falling behind while waiting for proof nobody has yet |

- Webinar poll: **63% win first.** Bain sits in the win first camp.
- **Win first still needs a measurement plan.** Be deliberate about data infrastructure now, so it can
  later feed the ROI methods CFOs are used to.
- To pitch a prove first boss: be honest about which data is reliable and which is sketchy. Agree as a
  management team on how to judge it. If the bar is ROI, nobody is there yet, and waiting is a risk too.

## 6. Signs the channel works, even when attribution can't see it

- Across third-party studies, **AI search traffic converts 4-23x better** than traditional organic. Most
  studies land at 4-10x. The likely reason is more time and more back-and-forth in the model before the
  click.
- **Referral traffic undercounts AI.** "How did you hear about us" (HDYHAU) surveys and clickstream data
  show a big multiplier over direct referral numbers. The gap varies by industry.
- ChatGPT has been inconsistent with UTM tags, so a lot of AI traffic shows up as **direct**.
- What win first teams combine: share of voice behind competitors, organic traffic falling, and a spike in
  signups or sales from unknown or branded sources. Together they show the channel working, even without
  per-lever attribution.

## 7. AI search sits between brand and performance

- Bain x Google B2B study: **80% of buyers have a day one vendor list, and about 90% buy from it.** Day one
  lists are driven by word of mouth, owned sites, industry sites and review sites. AI search is driven by
  the same things.
- So "brand" levers like reputation, reviews and PR now drive discoverability directly. The funnel
  collapses. You win by genuinely being the right answer, not by out-spending everyone on performance.
- One brand definition shared on the panel: brand is what people say about you when you're not in the
  room. An LLM does exactly that.
- **Org design:** AEO/GEO needs owned web, social, PR, paid media, SEO and paid search working together.
  Build a **cross-functional team**. Don't make it just another job for the SEO person.
- SEO skills now matter on paid AI surfaces, and paid skills matter on organic ones.

## 8. How to measure AI search, step by step

1. **Start with what you already have.** Don't throw out your attribution or buy a new system first.
   Don't analyse data you don't have yet.
2. **Build the prompt architecture.** Know your customers, products and market. Group prompts into topic
   clusters. Estimate how often people ask them, using many internal and external sources. Anyone claiming
   perfect prompt-volume data is lying. Don't just copy your keyword list across. Also use customer
   interviews, surveys, forums, social conversations and synthetic personas. Add persona, journey-stage
   and long-tail variants later.
3. **Set baselines in AI visibility tools,** then watch the leading indicators: **mention rate and
   recommendation rate.**
4. **Triangulate with sources you trust more.** AI referral traffic (directional, not absolute), CRM
   data, HDYHAU surveys, engagement and conversion on owned content, branded search volume and direct
   traffic.
5. **Use HDYHAU surveys to correct multi-touch attribution (MTA).** MTA over-credits paid touches, which
   often happen after an LLM has already taught the buyer about the category. The survey gap shows how
   much to adjust.
6. **Log every action you take.** More and more surfaces shape AI answers. To link action to result,
   you need a record of what was done and when.

### Media mix models (MMM) and incrementality

- An MMM needs **at least a year of data** and steady exposure metrics like share of voice, citation
  rate, sentiment and referral traffic. It will help later than people want.
- MMMs lean toward scale and reach. LLMs don't give reliable reach or prompt-volume data yet, so an MMM
  can't stand on its own.
- Don't ignore MMM. **Collect the data now** so you can model it once you have enough.
- **Incrementality test for prove first teams:** pick some prompt categories as a holdout and do nothing
  there. Fully optimise the others. Then compare every signal across the two groups. Balancing groups at
  the prompt-category level is hard, but it's the most rigorous test available.

## 9. Moves you won't regret

- **Owned content is the foundation.** It sets your authority with Google and LLMs. LLMs pull niche
  details from your own site, like product docs, local store pages and stock, especially for buying
  questions. Accurate prices and specs on your site also make third-party reviews and blogs more accurate.
  The direct impact looks small, but it drives the much bigger off-site share of what AI reads.
- **Build a cross-functional team** of marketing, analytics and a finance person. Give it one mission.
  Progress comes faster than expected.
- Visibility now moves quickly. You no longer wait weeks for indexing.

## 10. Q&A worth keeping

- **Prove first boss who still wants to win:** start small. Run one pilot on one prompt category, use case
  or customer segment, and see it through. Don't cast a wide net.
- **Rates beat levels.** Report week-on-week or month-on-month improvement in leading indicators, not the
  raw number.
- **"My brand shows up sometimes and not others":** LLMs are probabilistic, and memory and
  personalisation add more variation. You're trying to raise the **chance of being the recommended
  answer**, not hold a fixed rank. A single check by hand tells you almost nothing. Use many runs over time
  in a tracking tool.
- **AI in marketing beyond search:** content and media are the most common uses, with the smallest gap
  between leaders and laggards. Bain is most bullish on **agentic campaign orchestration**: less drift
  from brief to execution, double-digit gains in how well campaigns work, and four weeks of work done in
  four hours.
