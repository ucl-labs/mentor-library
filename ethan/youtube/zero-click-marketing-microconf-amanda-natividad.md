# Zero-Click Marketing: What to Do When the Click Disappears

**Amanda Natividad (SparkToro) at MicroConf US 2026, Portland**

> Demand-side companion to `frameworks/six-pillar-reputation-system.md`. Neil Patel explains what
> replaces ranking as the thing you optimise. This talk explains why the click disappeared and how
> to measure marketing once attribution stops working.

---

## Key takeaways

- **The alligator graph.** Impressions rise, clicks decline, the jaws keep opening. The problem is
  not that nobody sees your marketing, it is that more people see it and fewer click.
- **58.5% of all Google searches are zero-click.** For every 1,000 US Google searches, only **360
  clicks reach the open web**.
- **Search is a behaviour, not a channel.** Roughly one in five searches happens somewhere other
  than a traditional search engine, and the zero-click problem applies on every one of them.
- **Your homepage has changed.** It is now a search snippet, a ChatGPT answer, and what your
  customers and employees say on social. First impressions form across dozens of surfaces before
  anyone reaches your .com.
- **Attribution is structurally broken** (cookies, blockers, multi-device, privacy law, dark social).
  Attributed outcomes can overstate causal impact by 2x to 10x.
- **Traffic is a bad KPI.** HubSpot's organic search traffic dropped 80% while Q4 2025 revenue hit
  an all-time high and the stock jumped.
- **Search captures demand, public evidence creates it.** Your job is not only to rank, it is to
  influence the public record.
- **The banking model.** Roughly **five zero-click deposits for every one withdrawal.**

### The Dropbox incrementality study

*Source slide: Dropbox IEEE Access paper, March 2026. (On stage she said "published last month",
which would place it around July 2026. The slide date is treated as primary.)*

|---|---|---|

Both underwater causally. Dropbox reallocated **$25M** away from low-incrementality spend and
portfolio LTV:CAC improved **53%**. Attributed outcomes overstated causal impact by 2x to 10x.

### Why attribution breaks (four reasons)

1. **Cookies.** Only ~30% of users accept them; Safari rejects third-party cookies by default.
2. **Ad and analytics blockers.** 20-60% of browsers block analytics entirely, closer to 60% for tech-savvy audiences.
3. **Multi-device journeys.** ~3.6 devices per person worldwide.
4. **Privacy regulation.** GDPR, CCPA, LGPD make persistent tracking impractical.

## The frameworks

### 1. Build on rented land

> *For every 1 visitor to your website, 100 will get to know you on platforms you don't control.*

| What this means | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Publish native value on platforms where your audience already pays attention | LinkedIn & X (social feeds) |
| Write posts that teach without requiring the click | Reddit & niche communities |
| Answer the question directly in the feed | YouTube & podcasts |
| Show expertise in public, build trust before the visit | Newsletters in your space |
| | AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity) |
| | Industry Slack/Discord groups |

**No two companies' marketing mix should be the same. Even in the same sector.**

### 2. Keep one owned channel strong

Email. Open and click rates have barely moved in 20 years. The algorithm cannot suppress it and the
platform cannot hide it. Build on rented land, but make every rented-land effort feed the list.

### 3. Measure what actually matters

*Drop traffic as a KPI.* Traffic is a fine secondary metric and a bad optimisation target unless
you sell CPM ads.

| Layer | Content Platforms | Search | Social | Website |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | Followers | KW Volume | Viewers / Listeners | Returning Visitors |
| **Reach** | Impressions | Visibility | Views, Minutes | Email Subs |
| **Interest** | Engagements | Product Page Views | Comments, Shares, Votes | Branded Searches |
| **Sales** | *All the things above lead to this* | | | Conversions & Conv. Rate |

**Incremental sales lift is the primary KPI. Everything else is a leading indicator.**

### 4. Build a correlation dashboard

> You can't fully attribute, but you can correlate. Track leading indicators alongside business outcomes.

Worked example from the slide:

| Leading indicator | This month | Last month | Delta | Correlated outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn impressions (no-link posts) | 485K | 320K | +52% | Branded search volume |
| Email subscribers (new) | 1,240 | 980 | +27% | Demo requests |
| Podcast mentions | 4 | 2 | +100% | Inbound leads |
| Reddit mentions (organic) | 38 | 22 | +73% | Direct traffic |
| Newsletter open rate | 42% | 39% | +3pp | Trial signups |

*Slide note: "For us, zero-click social content is strongly correlated with signups over the
following 2-6 weeks."* (On stage she said "two to four weeks"; the slide range is treated as primary.)

Look for patterns over time, not single-touch proof. Reference case: Dreamdata's employee advocacy
programme drove a large increase in demos booked with no per-post attribution.

### 5. Public evidence creates demand, search only captures it

The real buyer journey analytics will never show:

1. Sees your LinkedIn post
2. Hears your founder on a podcast
3. Reads a newsletter mention
4. A peer drops your name in a Slack group
5. Googles your brand name and converts

Google gets the credit as "organic search". Steps 1-4 created the demand. So influence the public
record: reviews, forums, discussions, search snippets, third-party mentions, and the pages AI
systems cite. **If your strongest proof points are trapped in Slack, in finance reports, or in your
head, they are not part of the public record and they are not helping you.**

### 6. Content as a Service (CaaS): the 10-field brief

> If you and your internal client can't fill this out, you can't make the asset.

| # | Field | # | Field |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client team (Sales, CS, Product, etc.) | 6 | Desired action |
| 2 | Primary job to be done | 7 | Source material & SMEs |
| 3 | Audience (role, ICP, stage, objections) | 8 | Success metric + target |
| 4 | Desired asset type | 9 | Distribution plan |
| 5 | Moment of use | 10 | Shelf life & refresh trigger |

> *Stop optimizing for output. Start optimizing for outcomes. Content isn't a vending machine, it's a service.*

Every asset has a job and a client it serves:

| Asset | Internal client | Success metric |
|---|---|---|
| Case study | Sales | Win rate, cycle time |
| How-to guide | Customer Success | Ticket deflection, feature adoption |
| Benchmark report | Corp comms, biz dev | Backlinks, speaking invites |

At Fitbit B2B this shift meant **output went down and impact went up**, because sales came to the
content team first instead of last.

### 7. The Founder's Weekly Loop

> *What to do next Monday, not just what to believe by Friday.*

| Step | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pick 2-3 channels | Where your audience actually pays attention |
| 2 | Publish 1-2 zero-click assets per week | Standalone value, no click required |
| 3 | Repurpose each idea | Turn one insight into multiple native formats |
| 4 | Capture demand | Email signup, branded search, demo request, direct traffic |
| 5 | Review monthly | Correlation dashboard, **not fake precision** |

*Repeat weekly. Review the correlation dashboard monthly. Adjust channels quarterly.*

### 8. The banking model

Every zero-click post that delivers standalone value is a **deposit**: algorithmic capital, goodwill
with the platform, trust with the audience. Occasionally you make a **withdrawal**: subscribe, book a
demo, create an account. **Ratio is roughly 5 deposits to 1 withdrawal.**

> Don't make the click your only mechanism of value. Make the value obvious in feed. Make the action
> obvious with a link.

## Q&A notes (LinkedIn tactics)

- **Cadence:** post 2-3 times a week, not twice a day. LinkedIn is tamping down virality in favour of
  higher-quality engagement, so signal-to-noise matters. **Posting twice within 18-24 hours throttles
  your previous post.**
- **Personal vs company page:** a personal page always out-performs a brand page for raw engagement.
  Treat the company page as the record of news, and the personal page as point-of-view-led commentary
  on that news.
- **Video vs text:** match the platform's native preference. On LinkedIn an embedded video gets
  **8-10x lower reach than a text-only post**. If you are starting out, text is the lower barrier.
- **AI-generated content:** platforms are getting better at detecting it, and an AI-seeming post that
  restates the topic will not be scored as high-quality engagement. Use AI as an editor ("poke holes
  in this, where is it confusing, where do I need a clearer example"), not as the copywriter. If you
  use AI to make good-enough content you forget what good is, because the model is optimised to please
  you.

## Closing line

> The job of marketing is no longer just to drive the click. It's to create understanding, trust,
> recall, and preference wherever your buyer already is.

**Q: How often should a founder post on LinkedIn?**

Ideally you'll be doing this like two or three times a week. I think people used to think, "Oh, LinkedIn, you got to post twice a day." No, post less because LinkedIn, the way their algorithm works, is they're trying to find They're trying to give the right content to the right person. Um the other thing is, if you post twice within like 18 to 24 hours, your previous post is going to get throttled. If you're intimidated about LinkedIn, just think of it as like you only have to do it three times a week. That's it.

**Q: Is it better to post as a person or as a business profile?**

It depends, right? If you are just looking for raw engagement, a person's page will always perform better. Like people want to engage with people, not brands. Um so, the way I do it is I treat the company page as sort of like the news feed. Everything the company does just goes on that page. And then my personal page is a little bit more of my perspective of that company news. That way you kind of look as the company page is sort of the de facto, like this is the record of things, and your personal page might be you don't announce everything, right? Like you don't talk about every single blog post your company does, but it's a little bit more point of view led.

**Q: Video or text?**

Depends on the platform. Use the one that the platform is video or text first, right? So like Instagram is probably going to prefer video and images. LinkedIn, like I've been seeing that I mean, obviously an embedded video will perform better than like a YouTube link, but I will see that my reach on an embedded video is like 10x lower or 8x lower than a text-only post. So I would say, I think especially if you're just getting started and you you just need to get it done, just do text because it's a lower barrier to entry.

**Q: Any recommendations on how to use AI to create content? Does it get de-prioritized by platforms?**

I see it as I think the platforms are trying to be smarter about detecting what is and is not AI-generated.
